Where I stand

Technology, people, and strategy, in one conversation.

Most delivery doesn't stall on the tech. It stalls when engineers, business owners, and leadership each have a different picture of done. I studied how groups with different interests reach agreement. I apply it on AWS now, the same way I did on Azure and IBM Cloud.

  1. Technology

    AWS now, Azure and IBM Cloud before that. Close enough to each to ask the right questions, spot risk early, and turn trade-offs into business terms.

  2. People

    I keep stakeholders aligned across teams, time zones, and cultures. Clear owners, honest updates, no surprises.

  3. Strategy

    I connect the weekly plan to why the project exists, so scope calls and trade-offs are made on purpose.

How I run delivery

A simple rhythm that keeps everyone on the same page.

Same rhythm on a team of 16 and on a single product owner. Light enough to keep, clear enough that nobody has to ask where things stand.

A roadmap only helps if the people in the room can see what comes next.
  1. 01

    Set the AI tools

    Put the right tools for managing the project into the templates already in Claude or Grokbot.

  2. 02

    Discover, then lock the roadmap

    Sit with the team and the client, and iterate until the roadmap ahead is clear.

  3. 03

    Start running

    With the roadmap and the tools in place, the project starts.

  4. 04

    Iterate every week

    Each week, get better at it. Keep an internal forecast of what will be spent and what allocation the project needs.

  5. 05

    Demo, then hand it over

    At the end of each cycle, present what was delivered, with demos. Before the project ends, take the customer's feedback and leave them able to carry it forward after my team and I are gone.

Projects

01Media · Big data

Scrum for a team building the audience picture.

I ran Scrum for a team of 16 on a media and television engagement. We built over 103 Power BI dashboards and the analysis around them, including a model that predicts audience.

With my project manager, I owned the project's financial planning and brought the numbers to the monthly forecast. When the client needed a clearer sequence of work, I built the roadmap in Mural. They used it in the decision meetings.

  • Retrospective

    Feedback from the team, and the actions we actually took.

  • Roadmap

    The board we used to decide what came next.

  • Agile metrics

    Lead time, throughput, cycle time, and a cumulative flow chart.

  • Product roadmap

    Stories and features by sprint, so the time for each piece of work was visible.

02Banking · International credit

A roadmap the product owner could stand behind.

I worked closely with the product owner so we understood what internal and external clients actually needed, then wrote the 2024 roadmap together: the stories the developers would take, in order.

I was asked to facilitate a cross-area effort on Resolution CMN 4966/2021, the kind of work that only moves when several teams share one plan.

  • Product owner partnership

    One picture of the solution, shared with the people building it.

  • 2024 roadmap

    Each story the team would take, written down before the year got away from us.

  • Cross-area facilitation

    Resolution CMN 4966/2021, with the teams that had to deliver it in the same room.

03Mining · AI operations

Demand, filtered, before it reached the developers.

The team kept several live environments running for data and applications. Requests arrived from every team and every channel.

I filtered that demand before it reached the developers. Once it was sorted, it was obvious which business areas drove the work, and which ones management should prioritize.

  • Demand analysis

    Intake mapped into objectives, key results, epics, and features.

  • One management board

    A proposal to replace the scattered queues with a single place to prioritize.

  • Metrics dashboard

    Where the bottlenecks were, and whether we were moving toward the goal.
